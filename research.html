Research

What I work on

Machine learning is now good enough to be asked engineering questions, and bad enough at arithmetic that we should not trust its answers on their own. My research builds systems where the model is held to something external and checkable — a numerical solver, a circuit equation, a test suite — so that the result is both data-efficient and verifiable. Most of this work is grounded in power systems, where being wrong is expensive.

Interests: agentic AI, physics-informed machine learning, foundation models, scientific machine learning, optimization, and smart grids.

Projects

  • 2025 – present · dissertation

    Tool-augmented LLMs for power systems

    Answering questions about distribution optimal power flow requires a solver, not recall. VeraGrid-Agent writes the feeder and DER simulator input, executes the open-source VeraGrid solver, and reads back only the relevant result sections, keeping the context compact and every claim traceable to solver output. On VeraGrid-MCQ-150, a deterministic benchmark of 150 expert-templated questions we introduced, no-tool reasoning scores 42.7–49.3% while the agent reaches 97.3–100%.

  • 2025 – present

    Agents that are made to prove themselves

    Two lines of work on holding generated artifacts to executable specifications. A test-driven agentic framework synthesizes deployable low-level robot controllers, alternating between prompt-level refinement and direct code editing in response to structured PyTest feedback, evaluated on 2D navigation and 3D Webots scenes. MathAgent, with Kishor Kumar Bhaumik, builds a SciPy-augmented benchmark for agentic scientific computation.

  • 2024 – present

    Physics-informed synchro-waveform analytics

    Inverter-based resources are hard to model from data because disturbance events are rare. Embedding the circuit relationships into the learning problem compensates for that scarcity: the framework estimates the IBR terminal current response from only a few network disturbance signatures, and in the harder case jointly identifies the transient dynamics and the unknown line parameters. Validated on real waveform measurement unit data across several sampling rates.

    Paper

  • 2024 – present

    Event detection in continuous waveforms

    Recasting event detection in continuously recorded IBR waveforms as temporal object detection on spectrograms. Short-time Fourier transforms per channel are stacked into a tensor, which exposes transient and harmonic signatures more explicitly than raw time series; a U-Net over that representation beats an equivalent-capacity raw-waveform detector on both single-phase disturbances and three-phase faults.

    Paper Code

  • 2023 – present

    Online optimization with latent parameters

    What if the cost function you are minimizing depends on parameters you cannot measure, evolving under dynamics you do not know? Using control-theoretic identification on gradient measurements alone, the parameter dynamics can be recovered in the original coordinates — and, under mild conditions, from only finitely many measurements. A follow-up handles noisy gradients via a Gauss-Markov reconstruction and an instrumental-variable estimator, with a bound on expected tracking error.

    L-CSS Follow-up

  • 2021 – present

    Distributed optimization for multi-agent systems

    Earlier and continuing work on coordination without a coordinator: a partially distributed economic dispatch algorithm that converges in fixed time while accounting for transmission losses through Kron’s model, algorithm fusion for heterogeneous online learning, and exact distributed attitude alignment on SO(3) for robot teams whose local objectives conflict.
